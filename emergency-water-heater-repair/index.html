Signs You Need Emergency Water Heater Repair in West Valley City

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Violent rumbling or banging from the heater as it runs
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor
  • Tap water hot enough to burn, even at the usual setting
  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging
  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life
  • Signs of scorching or backdrafting around the base or top of a gas heater
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater emergency stacks several hazards in one small space. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding water at high temperature and household pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, is doing so for a reason. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, tighten a leaking fitting until it cracks, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into a real disaster. The safest moves for a homeowner are the simple ones: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a few hundred dollars versus a new heater. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Tag the water heater supply valve and the main shutoff so nobody has to search during a leak
  • A drain pan with a proper drain line under the tank can turn a flood into a manageable puddle
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
