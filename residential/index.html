Clear Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Before we start any repair, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your heater's age and gallon capacity, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. Then you get a straightforward recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

A lot of water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Watch for discolored or rusty hot water, popping noises when the burner fires, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a spent anode rod, or a worn dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign usually costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The serial number on the label usually tells us. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth scheduling a replacement before the current one forces the issue. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Annual Flushing

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Each maintenance visit from West Valley City Water Heater is more than a quick drain and refill. The technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and sets a safe water temperature. Minor faults get caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and your water heater stays running at full efficiency.

Most homes do best with one flush a year, and houses on hard water often need two. We adjust the schedule to the age of your heater, how hard your household works it, and local water quality, which controls how quickly sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

The savings add up quickly. A tank without a sediment crust heats water more efficiently, a fresh anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Compared with the cost of replacing a tank early, regular maintenance is a bargain.

Proper Sizing

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Getting the size right is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before recommending a new heater, West Valley City Water Heater looks at the details that drive your household's peak demand: the number of people and bathrooms, showers that run back to back, laundry and dishwasher timing, and how cold the water entering the house gets. That gives you a unit with a first-hour rating or GPM capacity to cover your busiest hour without wasting energy on excess capacity.

Households change over time. A new baby, a teenager, or an in-law suite can overwhelm the tank that once kept up just fine. Tankless water heaters heat water on demand and don't run out as long as you stay within the rated flow, while hybrid heat pump tanks draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll lay out the real trade-offs in upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Most repair calls we take come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The shower goes cold after a few minutes, the tank pops every time the burner fires, or the tankless unit locks out with an error code. Add rusty water, sulfur smells, and dripping relief valves, and you have the full list. We fix every one of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element only means lukewarm showers. A relief valve spitting water, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and a slow leak at the base can quietly ruin flooring before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. Our technicians carry the parts to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
