Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • A second or third repair within a year or two

Why Professional Water Heater Replacement Matters

Consider the vent first. Gas water heater exhaust carries carbon monoxide, so an undersized, badly sloped, or loose vent can spill it back into living space. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and there is a lot to get right. Local code may also call for an expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps, and some areas want a permit. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
