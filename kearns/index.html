Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Kearns

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
